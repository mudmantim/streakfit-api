# Brain Boost & Insights — independent quality review

**Date:** 2026-09-19 · **Scope:** all 479 entries (192 Brain Boost questions, 287 insights)
**Inventory:** `docs/content/inventory.json` — every entry with a stable content-hash id, so a
claim can be pointed at in a conversation and still be findable after an edit.

No new content was written for this review. Everything below is a defect found in what
already existed, or a claim checked and left alone.

---

## 1. How the 479 were triaged

Verifying 479 entries individually against sources is neither possible nor useful — most of
them make no checkable claim. Each entry was classified by what kind of statement it makes:

| | count | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Factual** — a number, a superlative, a mechanism, or an absolute | 178 | 37% |
| Editorial encouragement — true-ish by construction, nothing to verify | 37 | 7% |
| Other — descriptive, already hedged, or a definition | 264 | 55% |

Of the 178 factual claims, **175 already carry a hedge** ("tends to", "most people",
"roughly", "still argued over"). The review queue was the **96 unhedged factual claims**, and
within those the **24 that assert a number or a superlative** were checked first, because
those are the ones a child repeats and an adult can look up.

**This is a confidence classification, not a verification of all 479.** What was verified
against sources is listed in §2. Everything else in the factual bucket was read and judged,
which is a weaker thing and is described as such.

## 2. Claims checked against sources

### Checked and CHANGED

**Sneeze velocity** — *"something like 10 to 20 miles an hour"*
The literature does not agree with itself. Tang et al. report a maximum ~4.5 m/s (≈10 mph)
from plume imaging; particle-image-velocimetry work reports ~15.9 m/s (≈35 mph). The previous
text picked the low end and stated it as settled. Rewritten so the **disagreement** is the
fact, which is both honest and more interesting than either number. The 100 mph figure traces
to a 1940s measurement error, and that stays because it is the useful part.
· [PIV measurements (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8663001/)
· [How forceful is a sneeze? (Popular Science)](https://www.popsci.com/science/article/2013-08/fyi-how-forceful-sneeze/)

**"The first minute of anything physical feels the hardest"**
The mechanism was right and the numbers were not. Oxygen uptake takes roughly **1–3 minutes**
to reach steady state, not one minute, and "anything physical" is wrong at the other end — a
long hard effort gets harder later, not easier. Now "the first few minutes", with the
oxygen-lag explanation intact.
· [Oxygen uptake kinetics during exercise (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10368878/)
· [VO2 kinetics explained](https://www.highnorth.co.uk/articles/vo2-kinetics-cycling)

**Tooth enamel — "the one part of you with no repair crew"**
The cellular claim is correct: enamel is acellular, ameloblasts die off once the tooth forms,
and it cannot biologically regenerate. But the phrasing implied nothing can be done, and
**remineralisation is real** — minerals from saliva and toothpaste move back into enamel that
has started to soften, which is most of what brushing is for. A true statement that left out
the part a child can act on. Explanation extended.
· [Salivary remineralisation of enamel (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7076334/)
· [Analysis of dental enamel remineralization (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10135549/)

**Human endurance running** — *"among the best… almost nothing else can keep going as far"*
Under-stated rather than wrong: the comparative evidence supports humans being **the** best
distance runners among animals. The change is that it now says **why** — we sweat, and
animals that gallop cannot pant while galloping — which was the interesting half and was
missing.
· [Endurance running hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endurance_running_hypothesis)
· [Humans are born to run (UC Davis)](https://www.ucdavis.edu/blog/humans-are-born-run)
· [Humans: hot, sweaty, natural-born runners (Harvard Gazette)](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2007/04/humans-hot-sweaty-natural-born-runners/)

### Checked and LEFT ALONE

- **Gluteus maximus is the largest muscle** — standard and correct.
- **Achilles is the largest tendon** — correct.
- **"A muscle can only pull"** — correct, and the reason muscles come in pairs.
- **Blood vessels "tens of thousands of miles"** — the ~60,000-mile textbook estimate; the
  vague phrasing is appropriate for a figure that is itself an estimate.
- **Vitamin D is the only vitamin made from daylight** — correct as stated.
- **Femur is the longest and strongest bone, and slightly curved** — correct.
- **Deep-sleep waste clearance** — already written as contested, which it is.

## 3. Defects found by structure, not by sources

### 3.1 Answerable from the grammar — 9 questions

The tell the length tests could not see. When three distractors open with the same word and
the answer does not, the answer is the odd one out and no knowledge is needed to find it.

| | |
|---|---|
| "What is reaction time?" | three options began *How…*, the answer began *The…* |
| "What's the largest organ in the human body?" | three began *The…*, the answer was *Skin* |
| "Why do 'all or nothing' plans often fall apart?" | three began *They…*, the answer did not |
| + six more with *It…* / *Because…* | |

All nine rewritten. **Every one of them passed the position and length checks**, which is
worth stating plainly: the guards added in the previous pass were necessary and not
sufficient.

### 3.2 A question that answered itself — 1

*"What's wrong with 'no pain, no gain'?"* asserted that something was wrong with it, which
ruled out the option saying nothing was. Re-asked neutrally: *"How much does getting fitter
depend on a session hurting?"*

### 3.3 Near-duplicates — 3 pairs, across the whole 479

- Two insights about promises made to other people, almost word for word → one removed.
- Two questions about breaking up long stretches of sitting → the weaker framing removed.
- A lung fact appearing as both a question and an insight → the insight now carries the lobe
  count, which the question does not.

### 3.4 Checked and found to be false positives

- **"The explanation only restates the answer"** flagged 2. Both are fine: the tendon answer
  adds the Achilles, the blood-vessel answer adds the capillary. Left alone.
- **"Adult-framed assumptions"** flagged 37 on a first pass, 34 of which were the word *work*
  in its ordinary sense ("muscles work"). The real count was 3, of which one — *"Feeling
  tired sometimes just means your body needs water, not caffeine"* — assumed a reader who
  reaches for coffee and overstated thirst as a cause of tiredness. Rewritten.
- **Technical vocabulary** flagged 7 (*proprioception*, *cerebellum*, *isometric*…). Every one
  is an entry that **defines** the word, which is how a word should be introduced. Left alone.
- **Unsafe phrasing** flagged 2. Both intentional: one is a breath-holding distractor whose
  explanation says it is dangerous in water, the other is correct guidance not to hold your
  breath in a stretch. Left alone.

## 4. What remains uncertain

- **33 sentences run to 30+ words**, almost all in explanations — the text read *after*
  answering, where a longer sentence is more defensible than in a tappable option. Not
  changed, because a mass rewrite for a reading-level proxy is the kind of edit that makes
  content worse while the metric improves.
- **The 264 "other" entries were not source-checked.** They make no numeric or mechanistic
  claim, so the risk is low, but low is not zero and this review does not claim otherwise.
- **The remaining 72 unhedged factual claims** (96 minus the 24 numeric/superlative) were read
  and judged, not looked up. Reading is weaker than checking and is recorded as such.
- **Nothing here has been read by a person who is nine.** Every judgement about age
  appropriateness in this document is an adult's guess about a child, which is exactly the
  kind of judgement usability testing exists to correct.

## 5. Guards added

Three tests, all of which fail on the defects above and none of which define good content:

- `test_no_question_is_answerable_from_the_grammar_alone`
- `test_no_question_asserts_the_answer_in_the_asking`
- `test_no_two_entries_say_the_same_thing`

**Bias metrics after this pass** (unchanged from the previous one, confirming the edits did
not trade one tell for another): correct option is the strict longest in 38% of questions
(chance is 25%), mean length advantage +0.1 characters, one question where it stands out by
more than eight characters, answer position spread 49/52/47/44.
